PERMIONICS MEMBRANES · WATER TREATMENT ENGINEERINGTECHNICAL REPORT-MODULE 8Normalized Membrane Performance DeclineProjectorDynamic Log-Linear Regression & Standard Scenario Rates - Formulas, Logic & MethodologyBased on ASTM D4516-00 · DuPont FilmTec Technical Manual · Ordinary Least Squares RegressionDOCUMENT TYPETechnical ReportDEGRADATION MODELDynamic + StandardSTANDARDASTM D4516-0001PURPOSE & BACKGROUNDThe Normalized Membrane Performance Decline Projector is Module 8 of the Prototype Membrane Projection Software developed for Permionics Membranes. Its purpose is to model how membrane performance changes over time and to estimate when membrane replacement will become necessary, either from real historical plant data or from standard engineering assumptions when historical data is unavailable.This module implements two parallel degradation rate approaches. The first is a dynamic model that applies log-linear regression to actual historical NPF readings entered in monthly intervals, computing the site-specific degradation rate from observed plant behaviour. The second is a standard scenario fallback that uses three pre-defined annual decline rates (Conservative 5%, Moderate 7.5%, Aggressive 10%) when historical data is not available.Regardless of the rate source, the same normalization methodology is applied: measured or projected permeate flows are corrected for temperature variation (via the Temperature Correction Factor, TCF) and operating pressure variation (via the Net Driving Pressure ratio, NDP), isolating true membrane aging from operational noise. This methodology is defined in ASTM D4516-00 and the DuPont FilmTec Technical Manual.Why two approaches?A client visiting for the first time has no historical data, the standard scenario rates give them a credible preliminary projection for budgeting and planning. A client operating an existing RO plant has real NPF records, the dynamic model extracts their actual degradation rate and gives them a site-specific, regression-backed replacement timeline. Both outputs from the same module.02INPUT PARAMETERSThe module collects inputs across three categories: reference commissioning conditions, actual site operating conditions, and the degradation rate source. All subsequent projections are applied to this baseline.3.1 Temperature Correction Factor (TCF)Water viscosity decreases with rising temperature, increasing permeate flow independently of membrane condition. TCF quantifies this effect. A higher actual temperature means more flow per unit pressure. The TCF correction removes this inflation from the NPF calculation.TCF  TCF = exp [ U × ( 1/T_ref − 1/T_act ) ]T_ref and T_act in Kelvin (K = °C + 273.15)Constant UValueConditionU2640When T_act < 25°C — steeper viscosity-temperature gradient below referenceU3020When T_act ≥ 25°C — shallower gradient at and above reference temperatureTCF exampleT_ref = 25°C (298.15 K), T_act = 30°C (303.15 K): TCF_act = exp [3020 × (1/298.15 − 1/303.15)] = 1.1817. The membrane produces 18.2% more flow at 30°C than at 25°C for the same NDP. Without TCF correction this would appear as improved performance when the membrane condition is unchanged.3.2 Osmotic Pressure & NDPNet Driving Pressure is the true thermodynamic driving force for permeation - the hydraulic pressure available after subtracting osmotic resistance and all back-pressures. Osmotic pressure is estimated from feed TDS using the brackish water approximation.π_feed  π_feed (bar) = 0.7 × TDS / 1000 (or) calculated during feed inputValid for NaCl-dominant brackish water up to approximately 10,000 mg/L TDS (0.7*TDS/1000)π_avg  π_avg = π_feed × (2 − R) / (2 × (1 − R))Average osmotic pressure across the element — R = fractional recoveryNDP  NDP = P_feed − ΔP/2 − P_back − π_avgAll terms in bar - represents true effective driving pressureTermSymbolPhysical MeaningFeed pressureP_feedTotal hydraulic pressure applied at the feed inletPressure drop correction−ΔP/2Mid-point pressure represents average feed-side pressure across element arrayBack pressure−P_backSubtracts permeate-side hydraulic pressure from pressurized permeate linesOsmotic resistance−π_avgThermodynamic resistance to permeation from dissolved salts — increases with TDS and recovery3.3 Baseline Normalized Permeate Flow (NPF)NPF_baseline is the central metric - the equivalent permeate flow the membrane would produce if operating at exactly the reference temperature and reference NDP. Each scenario is defined by a specific combination of pre-treatment quality, operating conditions, and maintenance regime. The engineer selects the scenario that best represents the client's operating reality.4.1 Compound Decay FunctionPerformance decline follows a compound decay model. Each year the NPF declines by the degradation rate r applied to the previous year's value, not the original baseline. This mirrors real membrane behaviour where the relative fouling rate on an already-fouled membrane is proportionally similar to a cleaner membrane.NPF(n)  NPF(n) = NPF_baseline × (1 − r)ⁿr = annual degradation rate (decimal), n = year number4.2 Standard Scenario DefinitionsScenarioRateGoverning ConditionsConservative5% / yrFeed SDI < 3, turbidity < 0.5 NTU. This produces a regression-backed rate that reflects the plant's actual behaviour rather than a generic engineering assumption.5.1 Mathematical FoundationThe compound decay function NPF(t) = NPF₀ × (1−r) ^t is non-linear in its natural form. Taking the natural logarithm of both sides transforms it into a linear equation, which can then be solved analytically using standard OLS regression.Starting from the decay function:Step 1 - Transform  ln(NPF(t)) = ln(NPF₀) + t × ln(1 − r)This is now in the linear form y = b + m × t, where y = ln(NPF), b = ln(NPF₀), m = ln(1−r)With historical data points (t₁, NPF₁), (t₂, NPF₂) ... (tₙ, NPFₙ) entered in months and converted to years (t_yr = t_months / 12), the linear model y = b + mt is fitted using OLS to find the slope m that minimises the sum of squared residuals.Step 2 - OLS Slope  m = Σ[(tᵢ − t̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)] / Σ[(tᵢ − t̄)²]t̄ = mean time, ȳ = mean of ln(NPF). Intercept: b = ȳ − m × t̄The slope m equals ln(1−r).
