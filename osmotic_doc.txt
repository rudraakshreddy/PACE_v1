Osmotic Coefficients of Various Water Types
This document summarizes the osmotic coefficients for various water sources commonly analyzed in water treatment and membrane science (e.g., Reverse Osmosis design).
1. Thermodynamic Osmotic Coefficients (φ)
The thermodynamic osmotic coefficient (φ) accounts for the non-ideal behavior of solutions with high ionic strength. In perfectly ideal, highly dilute solutions, φ = 1.0. As salinity increases, the coefficient drops. The following table provides standard literature values at 25°C:

Note: For highly dilute sources (Tap, Well, and most Wastewater Reuse), engineers generally assume φ = 1 when calculating osmotic pressure using the extended van 't Hoff equation.
2. Empirical Osmotic Pressure Rule of Thumb
In industry practice, membrane manufacturers often use a simplified linear coefficient based strictly on Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) rather than calculating individual ion molalities.
Standard Approximation: 1,000 mg/L of TDS generates approximately 0.7 bar (or ~10 to 11.3 psi) of osmotic pressure.
While seawater deviates more from ideal behavior, this linear coefficient serves as the standard industry shortcut for estimating the osmotic forces in brackish, well, tap, and wastewater streams.
